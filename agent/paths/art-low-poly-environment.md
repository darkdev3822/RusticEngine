# Art Path: a low-poly environment kit (trees, rocks, a building, in the Studio)
Worlds are built from kits: a handful of reusable pieces (two trees, three rocks, a wall, a roof) placed many times with different sizes and rotations. That's how small teams fill big worlds, and how games like Astroneer and Valheim keep a consistent look.

## Step 1: The look of your world
**Design question:** What's the mood: lush and bright, dry and harsh, cold and quiet? What's the time of day?
**Why it matters:** Environments set the mood more than anything else. Decide the palette for the whole world first so every piece matches.
**Build:** A Concept Journal entry with the mood, 4-6 world colors, and references. Concept art helps a lot here.
**Done when:** You know what your world's ground, plants and stone look like.

## Step 2: A rock (organic shapes)
**Build:** Start from a sphere or cube. In Vertices mode, select and move corners at random to break the perfect shape. Flatten the bottom so it sits on the ground.
**You'll learn:** Organic shapes come from irregularity: big, uneven planes look better than many small bumps.
**Try it yourself:** Make a second rock just by scaling and rotating the first one differently in your scene. Variety for free.
**Done when:** You have a rock that doesn't look like a sphere.

## Step 3: A tree (stacking shapes)
**Design question:** Round and soft (oaks) or tall and pointy (pines)? Stylized trees are usually 2-4 simple shapes.
**Build:** A cylinder trunk (6 sides is plenty), then cones or spheres for the leaves. Taper the trunk by scaling its top face.
**You'll learn:** Few faces, strong shapes; separate color areas for trunk and leaves.
**Done when:** One tree reads clearly from far away.

## Step 4: A building piece
**Design question:** Build whole houses, or modular pieces (a wall, a corner, a roof) that snap together?
**Why it matters:** Modular pieces built on a grid (2 or 4 units wide) let you build many buildings from one small kit, and fit together without gaps.
**Build:** A wall piece exactly 4 units wide using typed values (S, X, 4, Enter). Inset and extrude a window or door.
**You'll learn:** Working to exact sizes, keeping the origin at a corner or the bottom center so pieces line up.
**Done when:** Two copies of the wall line up perfectly side by side in a scene.

## Step 5: Consistency check
**Build:** Put all your pieces in one scene next to your character.
**Why it matters:** Mismatched scale or detail level is what makes a world look like mixed asset packs. Same palette, same amount of detail, believable sizes.
**Done when:** The pieces look like they belong to the same game.

## Step 6: Dress a scene
**Build:** Build one small area with your kit: scale and rotate rocks and trees so no two look the same, cluster them (nature groups things), and leave clear paths for the player.
**You'll learn:** Composition: big, medium and small objects together; leading the player's eye with shapes and light.
**Done when:** A screenshot of the area looks like a place.
