# Art Path: your game's first music loop (in the Studio's Music maker)
Music tells players how to feel before anything happens on screen. You'll make a short looping track for one place in your game (a town, a dungeon, a menu), and learn the building blocks every composer uses: key and scale, rhythm, bass, chords and melody.

## Step 1: Pick the place and the feeling
**Design question:** Which place in your game is this for, and how should players feel there: safe, curious, tense, heroic?
**Why it matters:** Game music serves a place and a moment. "Cozy town" and "boss fight" need opposite choices, so decide the feeling before any notes.
**Build:** Open the Studio tab, choose Music, and press the preset closest in mood (Adventure, Dungeon, Arcade). Play it with Space. Write the place and feeling in your Concept Journal.
**Done when:** You can say in one sentence what this loop is for.

## Step 2: Key, scale and tempo
**Design question:** Bright or dark? Calm or urgent?
**Why it matters:** The scale sets the mood (Major = bright, Minor = dark, Dorian = bittersweet, Harmonic minor = dramatic) and the tempo sets the energy (under 90 BPM calm, over 140 urgent).
**Build:** Press Empty. Choose a Key, a Scale and a Tempo that match your feeling.
**You'll learn:** Keys, scales and BPM, the frame every song is built in.
**Done when:** The settings match the feeling from step 1.

## Step 3: The groove (drums and bass)
**Build:** On Drums, put Kick on steps 1 and 9 and Snare on 5 and 13 of each bar, then hi-hats between. On Bass (octave 2), put a note at the start of each bar. Play it. Try Swing.
**You'll learn:** Beats and bars, and why the backbeat (snare on 2 and 4) makes people nod along.
**Try it yourself:** Remove the snare. Does it feel calmer? For a dungeon, that might be what you want.
**Done when:** The drums and bass alone make you want to move (or feel uneasy, if that's the goal).

## Step 4: Chords
**Build:** On Chords, stack three notes two rows apart (rows 0, 2, 4) at the start of each bar, changing the bottom row each bar (for example 0, 4, 5, 3). Lower its Volume and set Filter to about 1500 Hz so it sits behind.
**You'll learn:** Chords and progressions: why I-V-vi-IV is everywhere, and how chords give a melody something to sit on.
**Done when:** The loop sounds full with drums, bass and chords.

## Step 5: The melody
**Build:** On Lead, click a short melody: start and end on a highlighted row (the home note), move mostly between neighboring rows, and leave rests. Repeat your first idea in the second half, then change its ending.
**You'll learn:** Phrases, repetition with variation, and why melodies land "home".
**Try it yourself:** Mute every track but the Lead. Can you hum it?
**Done when:** You can hum the melody after hearing it twice.

## Step 6: Mix and put it in the game
**Build:** Balance Volumes (melody clearly on top, chords behind). Save: the Studio exports a looping .wav. Ask Ember to add an AudioStreamPlayer with Autoplay to the scene for that place.
**You'll learn:** Mixing basics and seamless loops.
**Done when:** Walking into that place in your game, your music plays.
